RoverTech - Robotic Manipulation & Perception

ROS 2 Humble Gazebo MoveIt 2 OpenCV C++ & Python

1. Mission Overview & Constraints

Development of an autonomous interaction system for a robotic manipulator with a control panel, designed for the European Rover Challenge (ERC).

  • Competition Constraints: No GPS/RTK systems allowed. The rover must rely exclusively on local perception and odometry.
  • Objective: Visually identify the panel, calculate its exact 3D pose, and plan the arm's movement to operate levers and switches (main switch) fully autonomously.

2. System Architecture

The system architecture integrates physical simulation with the ROS 2 framework, routing visual data processing (OpenCV) through to motion planning (MoveIt 2).

3. Perception & Pose Estimation

The visual system relies on an Orbbec Gemini 305 camera mounted near the end-effector (configured via URDF/Xacro) to optimize the panel's view without generating kinematic singularities.

  • ArUco Detection: Integration of 2 markers (DICT_5X5_250) on the Gazebo simulated panel surface (via PBR textures).
  • Pose Estimation (solvePnP): A Python node subscribed to RGB topics extracts eight 3D-2D correspondences. Leveraging the camera's intrinsic parameters, it calculates the panel's transformation relative to the optical frame.
  • Depth Validation: The known URDF geometry provides the nominal position of the switches. Depth data (RGB-D) confirms the actual distance, refining the target coordinates for the end-effector.

4. Motion Planning & Control

The calculated pose is broadcasted to the navigation system via TF2. The system handles discrepancies between simulated time (/clock) and real time to prevent extrapolation errors in frame transformations.

  • MoveIt 2 & OMPL: Trajectory generation to reach the target. The system solves inverse kinematics (IK) accounting for the physical offsets between the wrist base (ee_base) and the gripper fingers (ee_finger1).
  • Pre-Approach Phase: Implementation of an automated safe approach trajectory, stabilizing the end-effector 8 cm away from the main switch to prepare for the final physical interaction.

5. Current Status & Next Steps

The entire pipeline, from visual detection to autonomous pre-approach, has been successfully validated in Gazebo. The source packages compile correctly, and the architecture is ready for deployment.

Next Steps:
